Click a Question to See the Why Behind It
"What's going on for you right now that made you reach out today?"Motivation▼
Surfaces urgency and emotional driver. Are they unemployed, underpaid, career-switching, or just curious? Their answer shapes everything else.
"Have you looked into any other programs or schools, or are we the first one you've reached out to?"Competitive▼
Tells you where they are in the decision process. If they're comparing, lead with differentiators — program length, hands-on training, placement rate. If you're first, they're early-stage.
"What does a successful outcome look like for you — what do you want your life to look like after the program?"Vision▼
Gets them talking about their future, not just their situation. Helps you connect the program to their actual goal — income, independence, stability, or something else.
"Do you have any flexibility in your schedule right now, or are you working around a job or family commitments?"Logistics▼
Surfaces schedule constraints early. If they're working full-time or have kids, talk about flexible options before they bring it up as an objection.
"Is cost something that's on your mind, or have you thought about how you'd fund the program?"Financial▼
Opens the financial aid door without pressure. If they haven't thought about it, walk them through options. If they have concerns, better to address them now.
Common Outbound Objections
⚡On outbound, objections often come fast and reflexive — "I'm not interested," "I was just looking." Don't take them at face value the first time. Acknowledge, then dig once.
Click an Objection to See the Response
"I'm not really interested anymore."Dismissal▼
Say: "Totally fair — appreciate you telling me. Can I ask what changed? You filled out the form just this morning, so I want to make sure we didn't miss the mark on something." Often this is a reflex, not a real no. Asking opens the door.
"I was just browsing / wasn't ready for a call."Timing▼
Say: "Got it — no pressure at all. Most people who reach out are at different stages, and that's okay. Since I've got you for a second, can I just give you a quick rundown of what the program looks like? That way you've got the info when you're ready."
"It's too expensive / I can't afford it."Financial▼
Say: "I hear that a lot, and it's exactly why we have a financial aid team — they work with students every day to figure out how to make it work. Have you ever filled out a FAFSA, or would it be helpful for me to connect you with someone who can walk you through what you'd qualify for?"
"I'm working full-time / I don't have time."Logistics▼
Say: "That makes sense — a lot of our students are working when they start. The program is built to be intensive but realistic. Tell me about your schedule and I can give you a straight answer on whether it'd fit."
"Send me information and I'll look at it."Stall▼
Say: "Happy to — what's most useful: program details, tuition breakdown, or a video tour of the campus? I'll send that over, and then can we set a quick 10-minute follow-up to make sure I answered everything? Otherwise it's easy for both of us to lose track."
"I want to talk to my spouse / parents / partner first."Stall▼
Say: "That's smart — this is a real decision and they should be part of it. Would it help if I sent over a quick overview you can share with them? And we could even do a three-way call or campus visit together so they can ask questions too."
Move to a Committed Next Step
Say"Ryan, based on what you've shared, I really think the best next step is to get you on campus so you can see the welding shop, meet an instructor, and get a real feel for the program. I have openings [this Thursday at 2pm] or [Friday at 10am] — which works better?"
Advisor TipOffer two specific times. "When works for you?" puts the burden on him and usually gets a vague answer. Two options force a yes/no and feel easier to commit to.
